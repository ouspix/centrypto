Expanded rally regression and dimension-reduction study
Frozen data cutoff: 13 November 2026, 20:00 UTC

Removing correlated duplicates helped slightly. Aggressive relevance selection
made held-out prediction worse. This research does not establish a stable trading
edge, even with fees excluded. It predicts the return after a rally signal under
the existing long entry/exit rules; it does not identify what caused the rally.

DATA AND COVERAGE

The archive now supplies 8,984,010 minute candles across three discontinuous
periods: April 11-30, May 21-June 9, and October 29-November 13. There are 3,166
detected alerts across 184 symbols, grouped into 3,016 signal episodes. Execution
and liquidity rules leave 1,742 candidate entries from 1,136 episodes on 37 dates,
versus the previous 107 entries. Immediate and continuation entries can overlap.

The original strict detector is preserved. A separately labeled broader cohort
adds 5-minute moves >=1%, >=2 prior-day standard deviations, and >=2 times median
volume, with the same breakout and BTC-outperformance conditions. Winners were
not used to choose alerts. The strict cohort remains separately reportable.
All 321 original execution rows, including slippage variants, reconcile exactly.

NEWS MAPPING

The complete account-scoped database export contains 11,477 documents, 2,752
analyses and 3,341 immutable event revisions. Availability runs from October 31
through November 13: 14 calendar days, not several months of news.

The mapping produces 7,404 asset-document links across 143 symbols and 5,123
documents. There are 3,254 links actually available before the replay entries.
It uses explicit cashtags, unambiguous exact-case tickers, conservative project
names and one name learned from an earlier structured analysis. Direct mentions,
project/platform mentions, and broad market context remain separate. Links retain
document IDs, source URLs, matched text, publication time and availability time.

All entry features respect the latest of publication, collection, availability
and alias-discovery time, and the historical revision available at that point.
Stories are deduplicated. Counts, recency, sources and topics use 1/6/24-hour
windows. Topics include listings, security, unlocks, buybacks/burns, regulation,
exchange flows, adoption, upgrades, macro, liquidations, ETFs and airdrops.

Topic labels identify mentions, not verified events. A buyback announcement is
not proof of execution; an unlock mention is not a complete vesting ledger.
Whale features are attributed reports from sources such as Lookonchain, not
complete on-chain flows, verified wallet identities, or exchange net deposits.
Manual review removed three generic MEME/sector-index matches. No MEME entry was
in the model, so that repair changed no feature value or evaluated outcome.

CORRELATION AND DIMENSION REDUCTION

The final full-history diagnostic starts with 634 candidate inputs:
  - 40 collection/availability metadata columns excluded from economic selection.
  - 33 columns lack sufficient observed history.
  - 14 are constant or nearly constant.
  - 547 remain eligible; 120 redundant columns collapse to 427 representatives.
  - 417 representatives fail the specified stable relevance rule; 10 remain.

These counts describe the final training set. Every historical fold independently
learns its own eligibility, correlations and selection using earlier data only.
Pearson and Spearman matrices are saved. Redundancy uses |Spearman| >=0.90 after
training-median imputation. For example, 60-minute spot taker-buy share and buy
imbalance have correlation 1.000: they encode the same information. Perpetual
15-minute and 30-minute realized volatility have Spearman 0.911.

Nonlinear relevance uses mutual information on training episodes, then grouped
permutation tests in three chronological inner windows. A feature must help in
at least two windows. The fixed shallow gradient-boosted regression can capture
nonlinear effects and interactions. Low linear correlation alone is not grounds
for removal. The final 10-input candidate contains:
  perp_range_pct_1m, perp_return_pct_30m, calendar_hour_sin,
  perp_range_pct_5m, perp_rv_pct_60m, eth_vwap_distance_pct_15m,
  spot_rv_pct_240m, perp_rv_pct_15m, btc_rebound_pct_60m,
  setup_continuation (entry-mode control).

The most persistent economic inputs were 1-minute token range, retained in 14/18
outer folds, and 60-minute token volatility, retained in 12/18. The final list is
not evidence that all ten generalize: their combined outer performance worsened.
OI/funding inputs did not earn stable retention. Sparse liquidity and whale
features cannot be declared irrelevant. News inputs did not pass stable retention
in either experiment. Exclusion means insufficient predictive support under this
test, not absence of economic or causal importance.

HELD-OUT RESULTS

Whole symbol episodes stay together. Training labels must be fully observable
at least 120 minutes before each evaluation fold. Features stop five seconds
before entry. Older folds are weekly and recent folds daily. Training weights
give each episode equal initial weight and a fixed 60-day age half-life.
Evaluation error weights episodes equally. No parameters or thresholds were
tuned against these outer results. Selection always means prediction >0.

Full-history walk-forward: 1,547 entries, 1,014 episodes, 18 evaluation folds.

chosen strategy. The reduced full-history average turns negative (-0.013%) when

NEWS-SPECIFIC COMPARISON

A second experiment trains only on news-covered recent history, so absence of
older news cannot be mistaken for evidence against news. It evaluates 304 entries
from 197 episodes over nine dates, November 5-13.

Allowing news inputs did not demonstrate incremental predictive value. The
relevance pipeline's error difference versus the no-news pipeline was +0.092
points, with an illustrative day-block bootstrap interval of [-0.167,+0.407].
Only nine dates support that interval. Neither pipeline retained explicit news
inputs; allowing them also changes which other features pass screening. This is
a comparison of whole selection pipelines, not a causal estimate of news impact.
The final news-only research fit retains two BTC activity/liquidity-proxy inputs
and entry mode. The price-impact input is a candle proxy, not measured book depth.

COMPARISON WITH THE PREVIOUS MODEL

On the same 45 entries evaluated previously, the old wide nonlinear error was
2.316 points. Expanded-data wide error is 2.177, correlation-reduced error 2.141,
and relevance-reduced error 2.154. The old historical-mean baseline remains better
at 2.091. The reduced model's ten selected entries average +0.630%, but removing
the two INIT entries changes this to -0.534%. This apparent improvement is not a
robust new edge. The matched slice was studied before, not a pristine holdout.

LIMITS AND DECISION

Retain the correlation-reduced research candidate for comparison. Reject the
claim that aggressively dropping to ten inputs improves the strategy: it did
not. Preserve the excluded features and removal audit for future evidence;
do not equate a failed relevance test with proof that a variable has no bearing.

The archive has large calendar gaps and uneven source provenance. The old SQLite
file reported corruption; exports instead use its intact imported PostgreSQL
copy. Older import code can include synthetic flat zero-volume candles. They
cannot trigger volume-qualified entries, but may affect historical lookbacks.
Valid OHLC arithmetic therefore does not prove a uniformly verified trade tape.
Recent results are shown separately. There is no complete historical order-book,
liquidation, exchange-flow, buyback-execution or token-unlock ledger in this sample.

Minute OHLC execution retains the earlier fill assumptions: observed positive
entry-minute volume, next-minute prices, conservative same-bar barrier ordering,
gap handling and prior-minute notional caps. Positive future entry-minute volume
is an ex-post fill-availability assumption, not a pre-entry predictor; rejected
or unfilled candidates are not scored as returns. No historical queue position,
true market impact, short borrow, or liquidation risk is reconstructed.
The signal closes at the next-minute opening boundary; using that opening price
also approximates the fill without reconstructing signal-computation latency.
The five-second cutoff applies to regression inputs, not a simulated live order
round trip. These inherited execution assumptions remain material limitations.

Audits passed for 1,742 feature cutoffs, 100,230 derivative timestamps, 788,278
spot candles, 3,254 news links, 392 active analysis references, 111,488 rebuilt
news feature values, 321 original execution rows, 27 outer folds and 162 nested
time windows. No episode overlap or future training-label violation was found.
Source correctness, sampling bias and prior research remain separate limitations.

Models, feature masks, prediction rows and source links are saved beside this
report. No trading settings, orders, collector configuration or databases changed.
